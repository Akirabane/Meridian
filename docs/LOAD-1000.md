**One night of measurements on one Windows machine, over loopback, against synthetic servers. They say what this proxy cost there, on 7 and 8 November 2026. They are not its performance in production, and they are not a promise.**

# 1,000 players on one proxy: the first campaign

The goal set for this campaign: a comfortable peak of about 1,000 simultaneous players on **one** proxy, with a range of 500 to 2,500 so that large servers can use it.

This page gives what the first night measured against that goal:
- what holds;
- what could not be judged, because the load generator, not the proxy, was the limit;
- what is extrapolated.

[BASELINE.md](BASELINE.md) went to 500 players. Nothing here was measured on Linux or on a real network.

## In short

| Question | Answer from this night |
| --- | --- |
| Light traffic (BASELINE's profile L), 1,000 players | **Holds.** 0 players lost; 1.54 cores; added latency +46 µs at the median, +191 µs at the 99th percentile; the same in two runs |
| Light traffic, 1,500 players | **Holds:** 0 lost, 2.39 cores, +54 / +309 µs |
| Light traffic, 2,000 players | **Not shown.** The load generator was at its limit, and 3 were lost. The proxy itself used 3.5 cores and was not at its limit |
| Typical traffic (profile T2, built from one real client), 1,000 players, servers that write once a tick | **No player lost, 6.2 to 6.7 cores of the proxy.** The latency could not be judged: the load generator was saturated |
| One hour at 1,000 typical players | **No leak, no drift:** memory slope 0.005 MiB/min, threads and handles flat, processor 618 % at the start and 617 % at the end |
| 100 players moved at once, among 1,000 | All 100 moved (in a run with arrivals spread over 10 s). The 95th percentile was 6.6 s, and the others' round trips rose to 47 ms at the 99th percentile: **not "comfortable"** |
| "Comfortable at 1,000 on one proxy"? | **Light traffic: yes**, on this machine with a margin of about five times. **Typical traffic:** it holds with no loss, but uses about 6.5 cores. On 4 physical cores (8 logical) it runs at 72 to 87 % of them, and on 4 logical cores it loses players. Whether its latency is comfortable is **not known yet** (section 4) |

## 1. The machine and the montage

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Machine | Windows 11, Intel Core i9-14900KF: 8 performance cores with two threads each (logical 0-15) and 16 efficiency cores (logical 16-31); 64 GB |
| Proxy | `meridian` from commit `d66fe6e` (release, line tables), started on its own and pinned to logical 0-7 (4 performance cores), K = 8. Other runs used K = 4 (0-3) and K = 16 (0-15) |
| Load generator | `meridian-bench load` in its remote mode against `127.0.0.1`, pinned to the 16 efficiency cores (16-31), then to 8-31 from 750 players on. It holds the bots and the synthetic servers |
| Sampler | A separate script on logical 8-15, once a second: the processor of the proxy and of the generator (by processor cycles, as BASELINE), memory, threads, handles, context switches, sockets; the console's `list` and `reload`, and the `/metrics` and `/healthz` pages, timed |
| Configuration | offline, compression threshold 256 on both sides, no connection limit, as BASELINE |
| Background | The first 13 steps ran while the machine was in use (a video playing). The others ran with 1.8 to 5.9 % of background load, read before each step. 1,000 players at profile L was run in both conditions and gave the same proxy figures |

**The traffic profiles:**
- **L:** BASELINE's own: 20 small packets and 2 chunk packets a second down, 20 up; a world of 169 columns at the join.
- **T2:** built from one real Horizon client through the proxy (end-of-session counts of packets and bytes): 275 small
  packets and 3.5 chunk packets a second down, 40 up; a world of 441 columns.
  - Its "20 % walking" is a hypothesis.
  - A bench world is 2.5 times lighter than a real join (7 MB against 17.6 MB).
- **"tick50":** the synthetic servers write a player's packets once every 50 ms (`--write-per-tick 50`), as a game
  server flushes once a tick. Without it, each packet is written on its own.
  - The tick50 series is closer to a real server.
  - Its added latency includes up to 50 ms of the tick itself, which is not the proxy's delay.

**The raw data** (31 steps, a CSV per step, the samplers' files and a summary) is kept on the machine that ran it. It is
not yet in this repository.

## 2. What was measured

### Light traffic (profile L)

| Players | Lost | Proxy processor | Per player | Added p50 / p99 | Through the proxy p50 / p99 | Threads | Memory per player | Login p99 | `list`, worst |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 500 | 0 | 0.77 cores | 0.154 % | +36 / +77 µs | 95 / 207 µs | 1,511 | 466 KiB | 104 ms | 2.8 ms |
| 1,000 | 0 | 1.54 | 0.154 % | +46 / +191 µs | 106 / 298 µs | 3,011 | 438 KiB | 217 ms | 9.0 ms |
| 1,000 (again, calm machine) | 0 | 1.55 | 0.155 % | +46 / +181 µs | 107 / 294 µs | 3,011 | 427 KiB | 431 ms | 6.2 ms |
| 1,500 | 0 | 2.39 | 0.159 % | +54 / +309 µs | 115 / 427 µs | 4,511 | 427 KiB | 646 ms | 22 ms |
| 2,000 | 3 | 3.51 | 0.176 % | not judged | | 6,008 | 428 KiB | 4.9 s | 1.35 s |

**The cost of a player is flat:**
- 0.154 to 0.159 % of a core from 500 to 1,500, against BASELINE's 0.27 % on a machine where the bots shared the
  proxy's cores;
- threads exactly 12 + 3N;
- memory 430 to 470 KiB a player.

**At 2,000** the load generator was at 97 to 100 % of its cores: the 3 losses, the login p99 of 4.9 s and the slow
`list` belong to that step as a whole and are not attributed to the proxy, which had room.

### Typical traffic (profile T2)

| Step | Lost | Proxy processor | Per player | Cores 0-7 busy | Generator | Added p50 / p99 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 500, no tick | 0 | 4.13 cores | 0.825 % | | 8.2 cores | +233 / +667 µs |
| 500, tick50 (two runs) | 0 | 3.28 / 3.30 | 0.66 % | | 2.7 to 3.9 | +83 / +14,889 µs and +64 / +15,123 µs (the tick: see above) |
| 750, tick50 | 0 | 5.06 | 0.675 % | | 6.2 | +3.8 / +14.5 ms (the tick) |
| 1,000, tick50, K = 8 (two runs) | 0 | 6.25 / 6.62 | 0.63 to 0.66 % | 72 to 87 % | 11.4 to 11.6 (saturated) | not judged |
| 1,000, tick50, K = 16 | 0 | 6.72 | 0.67 % | | 12.4 (saturated) | not judged |
| 1,000, tick50, K = 4 | **104** | 3.96 (its 4 cores full) | | 100 % | 3.7 | not judged; `list` up to 4.7 s, `/metrics` p99 6 s |
| 1,000, no tick | **211** | 7.36 (cores 0-7 full) | | 100 % | 13.7 (saturated) | invalid (negative) |

**What these show:**
- **Typical traffic costs about four times light traffic per player** (0.63 to 0.68 % of a core with tick50, 0.83 %
  without), and the cost per player stays flat from 500 to 1,000.
- **Four cores are not enough for 1,000 typical players.** Eight logical cores hold them with no loss, near their
  limit. Sixteen hold them with room.
- **With every side saturated** (no tick, K = 8), players are lost. That step is kept as a stress figure, not a
  result.
- **The cost of a packet is the main cost of typical traffic:** about 21 µs a relayed packet at 500 players (29 µs at
  1,000, saturated), plus about 30 ns a byte for compression.

### One hour at 1,000 typical players (tick50)

- **No loss.**
- **Working set:** slope 0.005 MiB a minute.
- **Threads:** 3,010 to 3,011 throughout. **Handles:** flat.
- **Processor:** 617 to 618 % from the start to the end.
- **Memory after the players left** (three passes, on one proxy): 25 to 30 MiB, 11 threads.

Nothing was kept, and nothing grew. The round trips of that hour are not judged: the generator was saturated (its own
99th percentile rose from 354 to 467 ms).

### Logins, waves and connections that come and go

- **Waves.** 1,000 players in 8 waves: 1,000 of 1,000 in, at L, T2 and T2 tick50. Login p99 was 217 to 537 ms at
  1,000 (431 and 537 ms on a calm machine), 646 ms at 1,500.
- **Churn.** Connections that log in and leave: 5,499 to 5,999 cycles at 2,674 to 3,136 a second, 0 failed, at
  500 L, 1,000 L and 1,000 T2.
  - A run of 10,500 cycles failed 3,099 times "out of ports": Windows' range of 16,384 ephemeral ports, a limit of
    the machine and not of the proxy.
  - The churn steps were then cut to about 5,000 cycles.

### The console and the pages, under load

- **`list`:** answered in at most 3 to 9 ms at 500 and 1,000 L, and 83 to 131 ms at 1,000 T2 tick50. Up to 1.5 s and
  4.7 s in the saturated steps.
- **`/metrics`:** p99 about 100 to 133 ms.
- **`/healthz`:** p99 84 to 104 ms, about 80 ms even idle.
  - That was the page's accept loop, which looked for connections every 100 ms. It is fixed since: 10 ms, one write
    with TCP_NODELAY (#151); a test of 20 requests now gives a median of 10 to 11.5 ms.
- **`reload`:** three times in each active step. Answered in milliseconds, and no player dropped.

### Moving players between servers

Moves had to run in the bench's local mode, which starts the proxy itself; the proxy was moved to its cores right
after.

| Step | Moved | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 500 L, 50 at once | 50 of 50 | move p50 1.9 s |
| 1,000 L, 100 at once (night) | 0 of 100 | before the moves, the others' round trips were already about 490 ms and 300 of the 1,000 players still had no world: the step asked its moves anyway (the bench is fixed since: it now asks none until every bot is in) |
| 1,000 L, 100 at once, again (morning, 1,000 connections at once) | 96 to 99 | 114 to 264 players lost at login (see "Open points") |
| 1,000 L, 100 at once, arrivals spread over 10 s | **100 of 100** | move p50 5.4 s, p95 6.6 s; the others' round trips p99 0.32 ms before, 47 ms during the moves |

**The proxy's move itself works at 1,000.** The night's 0 of 100 was a swarm that never finished joining (1,000
connections at once, all bots and servers on 16 cores), observed once and not reproduced: the morning's runs at the
same size behaved otherwise. **But 100 moves at once among 1,000 players are slow:**
- each move sends the player a whole world (about 2.7 MB of the bench's);
- 100 of them at once kept the proxy at 568 % of a core for a few seconds.

## 4. What is not known, and what is extrapolated

**Not known: the proxy's latency at 1,000 typical players.** The load generator, on the same machine, was saturated in
every step that could have shown it. It needs:
- a generator on a second machine;
- or one spread over more cores than the proxy keeps.

**Extrapolated (not measured):**
- **Online mode.** These bots are offline: no encryption. AES-CFB8 costs about 17 ns a byte sent (BASELINE). At the
  83 MB/s that 1,000 typical players received, that is about 1.4 more cores, on top of the 6.5.
- **A real network.** Everything was on loopback. A network adds its own latency and losses, and takes the generator's
  and the proxy's shared machine away.
- **Real players and real servers.** T2 is built from one client; a bench world is 2.5 times lighter than a real join;
  the packet kinds are not real ones (no scoreboards, boss bars, plugin channels).
- **Linux.** No figure here is a Linux figure. [LOAD-VPS-250.md](LOAD-VPS-250.md) is the only Linux run, at 250 on two
  cores.
- **Above 1,500 light and 1,000 typical players.** The per-player costs were flat up to there. A straight line above
  them is a guess, not a measure.

**So, for "comfortable at 1,000 on one proxy":**
- **light traffic:** yes, on this class of machine;
- **typical traffic:** the proxy holds 1,000 without losses, but needs about 6.5 cores of this desktop processor (more
  in online mode), and on 4 cores it does not hold. Its latency there is not measured yet, and moving 100 players at
  once takes several seconds.

The cost per packet (about 21 µs, mostly one system call and one thread wake for each packet) is where it can be
lowered. That is the next work: writing a read's packets together, and reusing the compressor. It is measured before
and after with the same bench.

## 5. Open points

1. **Logins lost when 1,000 players connect at once** (morning runs, local mode): 114 to 264 connections were aborted
   5 to 8 s after the start (Windows error 10053, "connection aborted by the software of the host").
   - Not attributed. It is before any time limit of the proxy (10 s of silence, 30 s for a login) or of the bots (30 s),
     with the proxy's listen queue at 4,096. It is probably the local TCP stack under about 3,000 sockets opened in a
     few seconds on loopback.
   - The test that tells it apart is to be made on a quiet machine: 1,000 connections that only do the handshake and a
     status request, and the system's TCP counters before and after.
   - With arrivals spread over 10 s, none was lost.
2. **The night's 0 of 100 moves at 1,000** was a swarm that never finished joining. It was observed once and not
   reproduced.
3. **A second machine for the load generator,** to judge the latency at 1,000 typical players and to reach 2,500.
4. **The steps of the plan not run:**
   - a server stopping during moves (A15);
   - the drain with moves on the console;
   - the logins at a steady 1,000 a minute (A11 as planned);
   - the heavy profile at 1,000.
5. **The `/healthz` fix (#151)** is to be measured under load in the next campaign.
